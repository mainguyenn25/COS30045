Televisions

Explore television energy consumption and understand how screen size and usage can affect electricity consumption.

Television Energy Consumption

Televisions are common household appliances in Australia. Their energy consumption can vary depending on factors such as screen technology, screen size, brightness and usage time.

Modern televisions are generally designed to use less energy than many older models. However, leaving a television running for long periods can still contribute to household electricity consumption.

LED TVs

LED televisions are commonly designed to provide good picture quality while using relatively low power.

Screen Size

Larger screens may require more electricity depending on the technology and model.

Usage Time

The number of hours a television is used each day directly affects its total energy consumption.

Example Television Power Usage

The following values are examples for educational purposes. Actual power consumption varies between television models.

Television Type Example Power Example Daily Usage
Small LED TV 60 W 4 hours
Medium LED TV 100 W 4 hours
Large LED TV 150 W 4 hours
Large OLED TV 180 W 4 hours

Tips for Reducing TV Energy Use

  • Turn the television off when it is not being watched.
  • Use energy-saving or eco modes where available.
  • Reduce unnecessary screen brightness.
  • Avoid leaving the television on as background noise.
  • Consider energy efficiency when purchasing a new television.
